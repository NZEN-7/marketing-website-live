A Standard Heat Pump Still Leaves You Exposed

Swapping a gas boiler for a standard hydronic heat pump is a step forward. But it doesn't solve the fundamental problem: your home needs heat most at dawn and dusk, exactly when electricity prices peak.

A standard heat pump draws from the grid during those expensive morning and evening hours. You've escaped gas, but you're still paying premium rates every time the temperature drops at night.

Thermal Dawn is different. Our system charges when power is cheapest, a free midday window, your own solar, or late-night off-peak, then delivers that stored warmth through your radiators and underfloor heating all evening, with the compressor off through the expensive hours.

What This Actually Means for Your Bills

Two things drive what heating costs you: the price of the fuel, and the hour you buy it. Gas gives you no control over either.

Gas prices have risen over 40% in five years and aren't coming back down. Every year you stay on gas is another year paying for an energy source with nowhere to go but up. A Thermal Dawn system moves your heating onto the cheapest energy on your tariff, for years to come.

In Hawthorn the gas meter is off the house, so the daily supply charge went with it. What the heating costs there now depends on when it charges, not on what gas does next.

Works with Your Solar.Works Without It Too.

Thermal storage is often explained as a way to use your own solar, and it is. But the thing it really does is buy energy when it's cheap and spend it when it's expensive. Your roof is one source of cheap energy. It isn't the only one.

No solar at all

The store charges on off-peak rates overnight or in the middle of the day, then heats your home through the expensive evening peak. You're still buying your heat at the cheapest rate on your tariff instead of the dearest.

Free midday windows

Retailers are now handing out free power windows, typically around 11am to 2pm, to soak up the midday solar glut. That is exactly the window a thermal store is built to charge in, and it's free fuel whether or not you own a panel.

A small or ageing array

Plenty of homes have 2 or 3kW that barely covers the base load in winter. The store takes whatever your roof gives it and tops up from the cheapest rate available for the rest. Nothing is wasted and nothing is required.

Solar coming later

Install now, add panels whenever you're ready. The system picks up the extra generation automatically, so the order you do things in doesn't cost you anything.

If you've been told thermal storage only makes sense with a big array, that's worth a second opinion. We'll model your actual tariff at the assessment and show you exactly where the savings come from in your case.

Frequently asked questions

Do you replace gas hydronic boilers in Melbourne?

Yes. We replace the gas boiler with our heat pump and thermal store, and in most homes you keep your radiators, slab and pipes. It's installed by your own licensed installer; we can recommend one.

Do you cover Sydney and Canberra?

Yes. We serve homes in Victoria, NSW and the ACT.

How is thermal storage different from a battery?

Lithium batteries store electricity, then convert it back to heat when you need it, losing efficiency at every step. Thermal Dawn stores heat and cold directly, so your dawn solar becomes evening comfort without the conversion losses. It's infrastructure built to last decades. (See "Why not just a battery?" above for the full comparison.)

How much does it cost?

To get what Thermal Dawn does with off-the-shelf parts you're buying two things: a hydronic heat pump replacement, and a battery big enough to shift that heating to cheap hours. We do both jobs in one install. Total installed cost depends on your home size, existing pipework, and electrical setup. Book a quick check for an equipment price.

How much will I save?

It depends on your gas bill, your tariff and how much of the system you take, so we model it for your home at the assessment rather than quote a headline number. What we can show you is measured: in Hawthorn the gas meter is off the house, and across both running systems the heat pump hasn't come on between 5pm and 9pm on 95% of winter evenings: 110 of the 116 we monitored.

Do I need solar panels already?

No. See "Works with your solar, works without it too" above: cheap off-peak and free midday windows charge the store just as well. Adding solar later? The system picks it up automatically.

I already have a battery. Does this still help?

Yes, and the two aren't competing. A battery is expensive storage doing a general-purpose job; thermal storage is cheap storage doing one job very well. Heating, cooling and hot water are the largest, most predictable loads in the house, and covering them thermally is far cheaper per kWh than covering them from lithium.

The practical effect is that your battery stops being the thing that carries your heating. It's freed up for lights, appliances, the EV and overnight backup, and it lasts longer for not being cycled hard every winter evening. Homes with a battery already are often the clearest case, because the expensive storage stops being spent on the cheapest possible job.
